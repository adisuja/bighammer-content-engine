Issue 5, We start with what's measured
Hi {{first_name}},
Every vendor in this space promises savings. Thirty percent. Fifty. Seventy-five. After a while,
leadership stops listening, and they're right to.
Most savings claims fall apart under three questions: what was actually measured, what was assumed,
and what was left out.
Why savings numbers are so often wrong
 Measured and assumed get mixed together. A real failed-run cost sits next to a rough guess
about right-sizing, and both are presented as "savings".
 The same dollar is counted twice. Moving a job to cheaper compute and right-sizing it are not two
separate savings on the same spend.
 The gaps are hidden. Contract discounts, cloud VM costs and untraced spend quietly drop out of
the picture.
Measured vs. Estimated · how we report
Measured: total cost, cost by product, traced spend, failed-run cost. These come straight from your
billing and run data.
Estimated: savings opportunities. Each one comes with the rule used to calculate it.

Four questions to ask of any savings claim (including ours)
1. Which parts were measured from your own data?
2. Which parts were assumed, and at what rate?
3. What's excluded: contract prices, cloud VM costs, untraced spend?
4. Could the total savings exceed what you actually spend? If the answer isn't an obvious no, something
is double-counted.
What good reporting looks like
 Reliability waste (failed runs) kept separate from optimisation opportunity. They need different fixes.
 Every estimate shows its method.
 Untraced spend reported as a visibility gap, never as savings.
How the assessment shows this
The offline report contains measured figures only. When you connect your workspace, the assessment
adds estimated savings opportunities, such as moving scheduled work from All-Purpose to Jobs
compute, Photon usage, serverless for jobs dominated by start-up time, right-sizing, and idle cluster
time. Each is labelled with how it was calculated.
