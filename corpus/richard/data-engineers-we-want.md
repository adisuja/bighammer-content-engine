# BigHammer.ai - The Data Engineers We Want to Work With

Source: Google Doc 1MojQdRKON0RhAyvmu1Hw7iqlAB0UGhW3cNyIJ0wcyEE (owner: Richard Lawrence), fetched 2026-09-30.
Use: company-page / Richard voice. Culture + positioning of AI as assistance, human judgement at the centre.

There's a lot of noise around AI: hype, horror stories and competing agendas. For us, the useful question is how data engineers respond to it, and where they see the opportunity to create more value.

We see two broad responses. Some engineers worry that AI will replace them or make their skills less relevant. That's understandable, but it can lead to resistance, with the conversation focused mainly on what could go wrong.

Others look at AI and ask: how could this help me do more? Could it take away the repetitive, mundane work and give me more time for the interesting problems? Could it help me become more effective and make better use of the knowledge I've built up?

That second mindset is the one we most want to work with. We're looking for engineers who are curious, willing to learn and keen to expand the contribution they make. People who see new technology as an opportunity to improve what they deliver, and who want to explore how it can help them.

The opportunity goes beyond completing the same tasks faster. It's about giving engineers the space to get closer to the business: to understand what people are trying to achieve, ask better questions and apply their experience to the problems that matter. That's where their technical skills and domain knowledge can have a greater impact.

For BigHammer.ai, our role is to help those engineers make the most of that opportunity. We want to build on their expertise and keep them involved throughout the process. There are real challenges with AI and automation, so human judgement remains essential. Engineers need to understand the context, assess the outputs and make the decisions that require their experience.

Automating mundane work is a sensible place to start. But our ambition is broader: helping engineers use the time they gain to solve more valuable problems, strengthen their understanding of the business and grow what they're capable of delivering.

So the engineers we want to work with are the ones who are ready to learn, challenge and experiment, and who want to use AI to increase the value they bring. Our job is to give them the tools and support to do that, with their knowledge and judgement at the centre.
